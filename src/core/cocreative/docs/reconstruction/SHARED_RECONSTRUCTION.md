# Shared Reconstruction

## Purpose

This document records observations related to collaborative reconstruction.

The focus is not generation.

The focus is reconstruction.

Reconstruction occurs when participants gradually converge toward a shared representation of an experience.

---

## Core Observation

Users often do not want regeneration.

Users want correction.

These are not the same thing.

---

## Mole Whack Phenomenon

Observed pattern:

A is incorrect.

User requests:

"Fix A."

Result:

* A becomes correct
* B becomes incorrect

User requests:

"Fix B."

Result:

* B becomes correct
* A becomes incorrect
* C becomes incorrect

This creates an endless correction cycle.

Observed experience:

High frustration.

---

## Preservation Before Modification

Emerging Principle:

Before modifying an artifact,
the system should preserve already-approved regions.

Correction should mean:

Preservation + Modification

not

Regeneration

---

## Approved Region Concept

Users naturally create:

* approved regions
* correction regions

Approved regions should become increasingly stable.

Each iteration should reduce uncertainty.

Not recreate uncertainty.

---

## Desired User Experience

User:

"Move the grandmother slightly right."

Expected:

* grandmother position changes
* everything else remains stable

Not:

* room changes
* lighting changes
* viewpoint changes
* character appearance changes

---

## Spatial Reconstruction

Users often remember:

* relative positions
* orientations
* spatial relationships

more strongly than isolated objects.

Examples:

* the corridor was parallel
* the wall was behind the corridor
* the grandmother stood here
* the dreamer was here

Many corrections are relational.

They are not object corrections.

---

## Emotional Motion Preservation

Movement contains meaning.

Example:

Running vs Walking.

The correction:

"She was not running."

appears physical.

However the deeper correction may be:

"The emotional quality was wrong."

Observed distinction:

Running:

* urgency
* pursuit

Walking quickly:

* joy
* recognition
* eagerness

Motion may preserve emotional memory.

---

## Reconstruction vs Interpretation

A recurring problem:

Systems tend to interpret.

Participants tend to reconstruct.

Participant:

"The corridor was here."

System tendency:

Generate a plausible corridor.

Participant intention:

Preserve remembered space.

Reconstruction therefore requires:

Preservation before interpretation.

---

## Iterative Shared Reconstruction

Shared reconstruction appears to follow:

1. Initial representation
2. Mismatch identification
3. Local correction
4. Convergence

The goal is not immediate accuracy.

The goal is convergence toward a shared experiential representation.

---

## Working Hypothesis

Future reconstruction systems should support:

* preservation tracking
* approved-region locking
* relational editing
* emotional motion preservation
* iterative shared reconstruction

rather than full-scene regeneration.

The primary challenge may not be generation.

The primary challenge may be preservation.

---

## Open Question

Can local correction occur without preservation failure?

Current systems often perform:

Correction
→ Regeneration

Users often expect:

Correction
→ Preservation
→ Local modification

The gap between these models may be a primary source of reconstruction frustration.

This question remains open.
