# Dream Reconstruction

## Purpose

Dream reconstruction attempts to recreate
a shared experiential representation
from extracted dream content.

Reconstruction is not interpretation.

Reconstruction is not symbolism.

Reconstruction attempts to preserve:

- scene
- space
- position
- motion
- emotion

while making the dream experience shareable.

---

## Structure vs Meaning Observation

An observation emerged during dream image reconstruction.

Dream descriptions appear to contain at least two different kinds of information.

### Meaning Information

Examples:

* grandmother
* reunion
* joy
* embrace
* nostalgia

These elements describe the emotional meaning of the experience.

---

### Structural Information

Examples:

* room layout
* corridor orientation
* wall position
* window position
* participant location
* body visibility

These elements describe the spatial structure of the experience.

---

## Reconstruction Bias

Current image generation systems appear to preserve meaning information more reliably than structural information.

Observed behavior:

Meaning:

* grandmother remains grandmother
* reunion remains reunion
* joyful expression remains joyful

Structure:

* corridor orientation changes
* room relationships change
* character positions drift
* visibility constraints disappear

The emotional narrative remains.

The spatial structure becomes unstable.

---

## Example

Requested:

* grandmother inside the tatami room
* grandmother positioned on the far right
* wife visible only from the waist up
* corridor parallel to the shoji
* wall and windows beyond the corridor
* no visible garden

Generated result:

* joyful grandmother preserved
* reunion atmosphere preserved

However:

* grandmother moved into the corridor
* wife became seated on the floor
* corridor orientation changed
* wall and window structure changed

Meaning was preserved.

Structure was not.

---

## Working Hypothesis

Dream reconstruction may require separate preservation mechanisms for:

1. Meaning
2. Structure

Current systems appear to prioritize meaning preservation.

However, experiential reconstruction often depends heavily on structural preservation.

Future reconstruction systems may need to explicitly track and preserve both.

## Canonical Update Process

An additional observation emerged during dream reconstruction.

Corrections should not accumulate as separate instructions.

Instead, corrections should be merged into the current canonical description.

Observed process:

Original Description

↓

Correction

↓

Canonical Description Update

↓

Regeneration

↓

Correction

↓

Canonical Description Update

↓

Regeneration

---

## Accumulation Problem

A common failure mode occurs when corrections are simply appended.

Example:

Original Dream

*

Correction 1

*

Correction 2

*

Correction 3

*

Correction 4

As corrections accumulate, the relationship between the original experience and the latest instructions becomes increasingly unclear.

The reconstruction process begins to drift.

---

## Integration Rather Than Addition

Corrections should be integrated into the canonical description.

Not stored as independent fragments.

Example:

Original:

"The corridor is behind the shoji."

Correction:

"The corridor runs parallel to the shoji."

Result:

Updated Canonical Description:

"A narrow corridor runs parallel to the shoji."

The correction replaces ambiguity.

It does not create a competing instruction.

---

## Reconstruction-Oriented Rewriting

Canonical updates may require rewriting.

The goal is not literal preservation of wording.

The goal is preservation of meaning and structure.

A correction may therefore be rewritten into a form that is easier for both humans and AI systems to interpret.

Example:

Correction:

"The corridor is not going away from the room."

Canonical Update:

"The corridor runs horizontally parallel to the shoji and does not extend into the distance."

Meaning is preserved.

Interpretability is improved.

---

## Working Hypothesis

Shared reconstruction may benefit from maintaining a single evolving canonical description.

Participants contribute corrections.

The canonical description absorbs those corrections.

Future reconstructions are generated from the updated canonical description rather than from a collection of accumulated correction fragments.

---

## Meaning vs Structure

Dream reconstruction revealed two distinct forms of preservation.

Meaning Preservation:

- reunion
- joy
- grandmother
- embrace

Structure Preservation:

- spatial layout
- corridor orientation
- participant position
- body visibility

Current image generation systems appear stronger at preserving meaning than structure.

---

## Dream Reconstruction Challenge

A dream may feel correct emotionally while remaining incorrect spatially.

Participants may therefore report:

"The feeling is right."

while also reporting:

"The place is wrong."

Both forms of preservation may be necessary for faithful dream reconstruction.
