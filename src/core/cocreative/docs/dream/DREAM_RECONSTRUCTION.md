# Dream Reconstruction

## Purpose

Dream reconstruction attempts to recreate
a shared experiential representation
from extracted dream content.

Reconstruction is not interpretation.

Reconstruction is not symbolism.

Reconstruction attempts to preserve:

- scene
- space
- position
- motion
- emotion

while making the dream experience shareable.

---

## Structure vs Meaning Observation

An observation emerged during dream image reconstruction.

Dream descriptions appear to contain at least two different kinds of information.

### Meaning Information

Examples:

* grandmother
* reunion
* joy
* embrace
* nostalgia

These elements describe the emotional meaning of the experience.

---

### Structural Information

Examples:

* room layout
* corridor orientation
* wall position
* window position
* participant location
* body visibility

These elements describe the spatial structure of the experience.

---

## Reconstruction Bias

Current image generation systems appear to preserve meaning information more reliably than structural information.

Observed behavior:

Meaning:

* grandmother remains grandmother
* reunion remains reunion
* joyful expression remains joyful

Structure:

* corridor orientation changes
* room relationships change
* character positions drift
* visibility constraints disappear

The emotional narrative remains.

The spatial structure becomes unstable.

---

## Example

Requested:

* grandmother inside the tatami room
* grandmother positioned on the far right
* wife visible only from the waist up
* corridor parallel to the shoji
* wall and windows beyond the corridor
* no visible garden

Generated result:

* joyful grandmother preserved
* reunion atmosphere preserved

However:

* grandmother moved into the corridor
* wife became seated on the floor
* corridor orientation changed
* wall and window structure changed

Meaning was preserved.

Structure was not.

---

## Working Hypothesis

Dream reconstruction may require separate preservation mechanisms for:

1. Meaning
2. Structure

Current systems appear to prioritize meaning preservation.

However, experiential reconstruction often depends heavily on structural preservation.

Future reconstruction systems may need to explicitly track and preserve both.
